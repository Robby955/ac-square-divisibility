\begin{abstract}
We prove ordinary Andrews--Curtis triviality for the ordered presentations
\[
G(p,s)=\bigl(u^2y^{-(2s+1)},\;uy^pu y^{-s}u^{-1}y^{-p}\bigr).
\]
when $p,s$ are natural numbers and $p\mid s$ or $p\mid(s+1)$.
This includes $G(2,s)$ at every nonnegative height. A fixed sequence enters
a root-conjugacy pair, where an iterated doubling identity gives two
arithmetic finishes. Each substitution is an explicit product of conjugates
of the other relator, which is restored after use. We formalize the quantified
theorems in Lean and export numbered paths for independent verification.
For the remaining family $G(3,3m+1)$, we give an exact reduction to a
companion-restoration problem. That restoration and the full square-family
problem remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and context}
Work in the free group $F=F(u,y)$, with ordered pairs of relators. Write
$R\AC S$ when one can pass from $R$ to $S$ by finitely many relator inversions,
right multiplications by the other relator, and conjugations. The rank is
always two. Put $\sol(R)$ for $R\AC(u,y)$. Conjugation by an arbitrary word
is counted as one mathematical primitive; the executable certificates expand
it into conjugations by letters. Multiplication by the inverse of the other
relator costs three primitives, including the two inversions that restore it.

\begin{theorem}[Divisibility construction]\label{thm:main}
Let $p,s\in\N$, where $0\in\N$. If $p\mid s$ or $p\mid(s+1)$, then
$\sol(G(p,s))$.
\end{theorem}
The proof constructs the moves without assuming that the presented group is
trivial. Thus triviality is also a consequence. The admissible case $p=0$
in this theorem is precisely $s=0$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:p2}
For every $s\in\N$, $\sol(G(2,s))$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}One of $s,s+1$ is divisible by two.\end{proof}

The relevant Miller--Schupp family is
\[
\operatorname{MS}(n,w)=\langle x,y\mid x^{-1}y^nx=y^{n+1},\ x=w\rangle,
\]
where $n>0$ and $w$ has exponent sum zero on $x$. Shehper et al.\ prove
AC-triviality for all $\operatorname{MS}(1,w)$, for all
$\operatorname{MS}(n,y^{-1}xyx^{-1})$, and for
$\operatorname{MS}(2,y^{-k}x^{-1}yxy)$ for every integer $k$
\cite[Theorem B; Theorems 2, 3 and Corollary 9]{shehper}.
Their substitution operation precedes the witness operations used here.
The $p=1$ slice enters their $\operatorname{MS}(1,w)$ class. Below we give
the root-family witnesses directly.

Substitution by conjugate products and transport under automorphisms are
established techniques; see Panteleev--Ushakov \cite{pu}. Lackenby's ordinary
AC theorem for thickenable balanced presentations gives a different sufficient
condition \cite[Theorem 1.3]{lackenby}. We have not determined how the entire
family here intersects that geometric class. Lean formalization of AC and
automated certificate checking also have prior implementations
\cite{zhang,sair}. Our contribution is the arithmetic construction below
and its formal proof. Priority for the underlying triviality classes has
not been established by the comparison in this note.

\section{Witnesses that compile to ordinary moves}\label{sec:witness}
\begin{definition}
A witness relative to a relator $r$, from $a$ to $b$, is a finite ordered list
$W=((g_1,\epsilon_1),\ldots,(g_t,\epsilon_t))$, with $\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}$,
together with the identity in the ambient free group
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witness}
a\prod_{i=1}^t g_i r^{\epsilon_i}g_i^{-1}=b.
\end{equation}
We write $a\xRightarrow[r]{W}b$.
\end{definition}
This contains more data than an equality in the presented quotient.

\begin{lemma}[Witness compilation]\label{lem:compile}
An identity \eqref{eq:witness} gives $(a,r)\AC(b,r)$, with the second
relator restored after each factor. A positive factor costs three mathematical
primitives and a negative factor costs five. The analogous assertion holds
with the two slots exchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a positive factor, conjugate the donor $r$ by $g_i$, right multiply the
recipient, and conjugate the donor back by $g_i^{-1}$. For a negative factor,
invert the donor before the multiplication and invert it again afterward.
Repeat in the listed order.\end{proof}

The witness operations have explicit factor lists. Composition
concatenates factor lists. Conjugation by $h$ replaces every $g_i$ by $hg_i$.
Given $a\Rightarrow b$ and $c\Rightarrow d$ relative to the same donor,
a witness $ac\Rightarrow bd$ is obtained by replacing each conjugator in
the first list by $c^{-1}g_i$, then appending the second list. To obtain
$a^{-1}\Rightarrow b^{-1}$, reverse the list, negate all signs, and replace
each conjugator by $ag_i$. Iterating the product operation constructs
positive powers; inverse words give negative powers. These rules follow
directly by multiplying \eqref{eq:witness}.

\section{Entry into a root-conjugacy pair}\label{sec:entry}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root}
B_p=y^{-p}u^2y^pu^{-1},\qquad
A_s=y^{-(s+1)}u^{-1}y^su^{-1}.
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}[Uniform entry]\label{lem:entry}
For all integers $p,s$, a route of 61 mathematical primitives takes $G(p,s)$
to $(f(B_p),f(A_s))$, where
\[
f(u)=y^{s+1-p}u^{-1}y^p,\qquad f(y)=y^{-1}.
\]
We have $\sol(G(p,s))$ if and only if $\sol((B_p,A_s))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The complete route is in Appendix~\ref{app:route}. After its first twelve
macros, which cost eighteen primitives, the pair is
\[
\bigl(y^{-p}u^{-1}y^pu^{-1}y^su,\quad
u y^{-p}u^{-2}y^pu y^{-1}\bigr).
\]
The remaining macros cost 43 primitives and end at the displayed image pair.
Each step is a free-group identity, independently checked in the accompanying
Lean files \texttt{ACDiagonalDescent} and \texttt{ACSquareP2Entry}.

The map $f$ is an automorphism: its inverse sends
$u$ to $y^{-p}u^{-1}y^{p-s-1}$ and $y$ to $y^{-1}$.
A homomorphism sends every AC step to an AC step, with conjugators mapped
by that homomorphism. To finish a transported solution at $(f(u),f(y))$,
conjugate its first relator by $y^{p-s-1}$, obtaining $u^{-1}y^{s+1}$;
right multiply it by the second relator to power $s+1$, then invert both
relators. For the inverse image basis, conjugation by $y^p$ similarly gives
$u^{-1}y^{-s-1}$; right multiplication by the second relator to power
$-s-1$, then two inversions, finishes. These explicit basis corrections and
reversibility of the displayed route give both implications.
\end{proof}
The change of coordinates in this argument transports a complete path and
includes its final correction; it is not inserted as an additional AC move.

\section{The two arithmetic finishes}\label{sec:finish}
\begin{lemma}[Iterated doubling]\label{lem:double}
For $p\in\Z$ and $m\in\N$, there is a witness relative to $B_p$
\[
y^{pm}u y^{-pm}\ \Longrightarrow\ u^{2^m}.
\]
The recursive list described here has $2^m-1$ factors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The basic witness from $y^puy^{-p}$ to $u^2$ has the single positive factor
with conjugator $y^pu^{-1}$; explicitly,
\[
(y^puy^{-p})(y^pu^{-1})B_p(y^pu^{-1})^{-1}=u^2.
\]
Start at $m=0$ with the empty witness. At stage $m+1$, conjugate the
stage-$m$ witness by $y^p$, then compose with the $2^m$-th power of the
basic witness. The resulting target is $u^{2^{m+1}}$. The factor count
satisfies $L_0=0$ and $L_{m+1}=L_m+2^m$, proving the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Power finish]\label{lem:power}
For any integers $k,e$, $\sol((B_k,y^{-1}u^e))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With donor $q=y^{-1}u^e$, the identity $yq=u^e$ supplies a one-factor witness
replacing $y$ by $u^e$. Apply its power and product witnesses to
$B_k=y^{-k}u^2y^ku^{-1}$ to obtain $u$. The donor is restored. Right multiply
the second relator by the first to power $-e$ to obtain $y^{-1}$, then invert.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Arithmetic root families]\label{thm:root}
For all $p\in\Z$ and $m\in\N$, both
\[
\sol((B_p,A_{pm}))\quad\text{and}\quad
\sol((B_p,A_{pm+p-1}))
\]
hold by finite witness constructions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $d=2^m$. In the first case, conjugate the companion by $y^{pm}$:
\[
y^{pm}A_{pm}y^{-pm}
=y^{-1}u^{-1}(y^{pm}u^{-1}y^{-pm}).
\]
Use the inverse-word witness of Lemma~\ref{lem:double} in the parenthesized
factor. This leaves $y^{-1}u^{-(d+1)}$, to which Lemma~\ref{lem:power} applies.

In the second case, the same conjugation and witness leave
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
q=y^{-p}u^{-1}y^{p-1}u^{-d}.
\end{equation}
Writing $v=u^{-1}y^{p-1}u^{-d}$, the exact identity $y^pq=v$ is a one-factor
witness relative to $q$. Its inverse-word and product witnesses replace
the two occurrences of $y^p$ and its inverse in $B_p$, giving
\[
v^{-1}u^2v u^{-1}=u^d B_{p-1}u^{-d}.
\]
Conjugate this first relator by $u^{-d}$. The pair is now $(B_{p-1},q)$.
Conjugate $q$ by $y^{p-1}$ and rewrite it as
\[
y^{p-1}q y^{-(p-1)}
=y^{-1}u^{-1}(y^{p-1}u^{-d}y^{-(p-1)}).
\]
The $(-d)$-th power of the basic degree-$(p-1)$ doubling witness changes
this to $y^{-1}u^{-(2d+1)}$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:power}. At each substitution,
Lemma~\ref{lem:compile} restores the donor, so the concatenation is a path
of ordinary rank-two moves.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
If $s=pm$ with $m\in\N$, use the first case of Theorem~\ref{thm:root} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:entry}. If $s+1=pk$ with $p,s,k\in\N$, then $k\geq1$;
set $m=k-1$, so $s=pm+p-1$, and use the second case. This includes the
degenerate $p=s=0$ through the first case.
\end{proof}

The same argument also proves $\sol(G(p,pm))$ and $\sol(G(p,pm+p-1))$
for arbitrary integer $p$ and natural $m$. Natural divisibility in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a convenient formulation with a nonnegative quotient.
The doubling construction can produce long certificates; no short-path or
polynomial-complexity claim follows from the theorem.

\section{Parameter consequences and the remaining induction}\label{sec:open}
Sign, period, and original-coordinate transports give further consequences.
One period $p\mapsto p+(2s+1)$ uses four factors of the
square relator. Together with sign transport, the elementary $p=0$ case,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, this covers the residues
\[
p\equiv 0,\ \pm1,\ \pm2,\ \pm s\pmod{2s+1},\qquad s\in\N.
\]
In particular all integer $p$ are covered when $s=3$. A finite
65-numbered-move residual seed, corresponding to $G(3,4)$, completes all
integer $p$ at $s=4$. These parameter transports and the seed are proved in
\texttt{ACSquareP2Families}, \texttt{ACSquareP3Seed}, and
\texttt{ACSquareP3Families}. The seed's 65 moves start at its residual root
pair, not at $G(3,4)$ itself; its classical primitive cost is 81.

For reference, write the original presentation tuple as
\[
P(n,a,b,c)=
\bigl(x^{-1}y^nxy^{-(n+1)},\quad x^{-1}y^a x y^b x^{-1}y^c\bigr).
\]
The quotient-two pinches and parameter shears transfer the
divisibility result to $P(2m,m,p,c)$ and $P(2m+1,p,m+1,c)$, for natural
$m,p$ with $p\mid m$ or $p\mid(m+1)$ and every integer $c$.
They also transfer the completed $s=3,4$ residue sets to
$P(6,3,b,c)$, $P(7,a,4,c)$, $P(8,4,b,c)$, and $P(9,a,5,c)$, with all
remaining parameters integral. The supplementary Lean files contain the
pinch witnesses and complete transports; these statements do not assert that
the entire three-parameter family at any of these exponents is solved.

Here is the precise limitation of the proposed extension. Put
\[
Q_{r,d}=y^{-(r+1)}u^{-1}y^r u^{-d}.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Residual equivalence]\label{prop:residual}
For $p,r\in\Z$ and $m\in\N$,
\[
\sol(G(p,pm+r))\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sol((B_p,Q_{r,2^m})).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use Lemma~\ref{lem:entry}. Conjugating $A_{pm+r}$ by $y^{pm}$ gives
$y^{-(r+1)}u^{-1}y^r(y^{pm}u^{-1}y^{-pm})$.
The inverse-word doubling witness gives $Q_{r,2^m}$ while keeping $B_p$.
All these ordinary paths can be reversed.
\end{proof}

For the next residue $p=3,r=1$, a two-factor substitution
and invertible coordinate transport give the equivalent pair $(B_2,C_{2^m})$,
where
\[
C_d=y^{-1}u^{-1}y^{-1}u^{-1}y u^{-d},\qquad
D_d=y^{-3}u^{-1}y^{-1}u^{-1}y^3u^{-d}.
\]
Witnessed halving yields $(B_2,C_{2d})\AC(B_2,D_d)$, but the outer
$y$-exponents have changed. Closing the induction requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restoration}
(B_2,D_d)\AC(B_2,C_d),\qquad d=2,4,8,\ldots.
\end{equation}
The formalization proves that \eqref{eq:restoration} for all these $d$ is
equivalent to $\sol(G(3,3m+1))$ for every $m\in\N$, using the known
$m=0,1$ seeds. Neither side of this equivalence is proved here. In particular,
the first missing restoration is $d=2$; halving alone does not establish it.
This is a target for further work, and even its resolution would still leave
other square-family parameters to treat.

\section{Formal proof and executable evidence}\label{sec:repro}
The formalization uses Lean 4.29.1 and the pinned Mathlib revision in its Lake
manifest. Its copy of the official \texttt{AC.lean} is byte-identical to
the SAIR source \cite{sair} at commit
\begin{center}\small
\texttt{99a65377c5c4f412cd9af7b8d31c41464a855736}.
\end{center}
The main statement is
\texttt{AC.MillerSchupp.SquareFamily.P2.}\allowbreak
\texttt{divisibility\_square\_solvable}.
The root witness identity and both arithmetic finishes are quantified Lean
declarations.
Logical-dependency reports admit only the usual Lean axioms
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
The conjectures are definitions of propositions and are never assumed.

The Python exporter specializes the fixed affine route, constructs the finite
witnesses, and expands them into the official ordinary rank-two move table.
An independent word interpreter maintains intermediate states, and the
unchanged SAIR verifier checks the final numbered path. The exporter also
handles the admissible zero case explicitly. The finite CLI has resource
bounds; those are not restrictions on the quantified Lean theorem.

The experiment uses 50 distinct pairs: the 44 distinct pairs
$(p,pm)$ and $(p,pm+p-1)$ for $1\leq p\leq6$, $0\leq m\leq3$; all
$(2,s)$ for $0\leq s\leq12$; and $(0,0)$. All 50 pass unlimited official
replay. Of these, 48 satisfy the golden budgets of 100,000 numbered moves,
10,000 peak total relator length, and 5,000,000 work units. The exceptions
are $(2,11)$ and $(2,12)$, which exceed the work budget.
The recorded construction time was 2.980 seconds and replay time 0.567
seconds on the machine identified in \texttt{receipts/replay.json}; these
are one-run measurements, not portable performance guarantees.

From the repository root, the reproduction commands are
\begin{verbatim}
python3 verify.py --lean
python3 experiment.py --matrix --output run/replay.json
\end{verbatim}
The README gives dependency setup and reproduction instructions.
The path exporter and the Lean proof are
separate implementations; identical complete witness lists between them are
not asserted. Nine regression tests cover all numbered move types, donor
restoration, the affine entry at signed parameters, witness corruption,
boundary inputs, and equality with all 49 previously recorded constructed paths.

\section{A complete affine entry route}\label{app:route}
In the following table, slots are indexed by $0,1$.
$I_i$ inverts relator $i$, $M_i^\epsilon$ replaces it by
$R_iR_{1-i}^\epsilon$, and $C_i(g)$ conjugates it by $g$.
An $M_i^{-1}$ entry expands into inversion of the donor, multiplication,
and inversion of the donor again. Apply the rows in order to $G(p,s)$.
Rows 1--12 reach the normal form in Lemma~\ref{lem:entry}; rows 13--51
reach $f(B_p,A_s)$. Thus the 51 macros comprise 61 primitives.
The table is generated from the same frozen affine route distributed as
\texttt{entry\_route.json}; it holds for all integer $p,s$.
