\begin{abstract}
Let $u,y$ be free generators. For natural numbers $p,s$, define the ordered
pair of relator words
\[
G(p,s)=\bigl(u^2y^{-(2s+1)},\;uy^pu y^{-s}u^{-1}y^{-p}\bigr).
\]
Setting both words equal to the identity defines the associated group
presentation. We prove ordinary Andrews--Curtis triviality of this pair
when $p\mid s$ or $p\mid(s+1)$; in particular,
every $G(2,s)$ is covered. A uniform entry reaches a root-conjugacy pair.
An iterated doubling identity then gives two arithmetic finishes, the second
of which changes the root degree from $p$ to $p-1$.

The quantified theorem is proved in Lean. Explicit products of conjugates
also give an executable construction whose exported paths are checked by the
official SAIR verifier. This construction can have exponential length;
no shortness claim is made. For $G(3,3m+1)$ we isolate an exact remaining
companion-restoration condition. That condition and the full square-family
problem are open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and context}
\paragraph{Definition of the square family.}
Let $u,y$ be the generators of the free group $F(u,y)$.
For integers $p,s$, define
\[
r_1(p,s)=u^2y^{-(2s+1)},\qquad
r_2(p,s)=uy^pu y^{-s}u^{-1}y^{-p},
\qquad G(p,s)=\bigl(r_1(p,s),r_2(p,s)\bigr).
\]
Here $G$ is our notation for this specified ordered pair of words.
A relator is a word imposed to equal the identity; imposing $r_1=r_2=1$
gives the presentation
\[
\langle u,y\mid u^2=y^{2s+1},\quad uy^pu=y^pu y^s\rangle.
\]
We call it the square family because the first relation contains $u^2$.
The parameters $p,s$ index the family; they are not additional generators.
For example, $G(2,1)$ has relations $u^2=y^3$ and $uy^2u=y^2uy$.
The AC problem concerns the specified relator pair, so throughout this paper
$G(p,s)$ retains those words and their order, rather than denoting only the
abstract group they present.

\paragraph{Allowed moves and solvability.}
Write
$R\AC S$ when one can pass from $R$ to $S$ by finitely many relator inversions,
right multiplications by the other relator, and conjugations. The rank is
always two. Put $\sol(R)$ for $R\AC(u,y)$. Conjugation by an arbitrary word
is counted as one mathematical primitive; the executable certificates expand
it into conjugations by letters. Multiplication by the inverse of the other
relator costs three primitives, including the two inversions that restore it.

\begin{theorem}[Divisibility construction]\label{thm:main}
Let $p,s\in\N$, where $0\in\N$. If $p\mid s$ or $p\mid(s+1)$, then
$\sol(G(p,s))$.
\end{theorem}
The proof constructs the moves without assuming that the presented group is
trivial. Thus triviality is also a consequence. The admissible case $p=0$
in this theorem is precisely $s=0$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:p2}
For every $s\in\N$, $\sol(G(2,s))$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}One of $s,s+1$ is divisible by two.\end{proof}

\subsection*{Original coordinates and the square relation}
The square relation arises from the original family
\begin{equation}\label{eq:original}
P(n,a,b,c)=
\bigl(x^{-1}y^nxy^{-(n+1)},\quad x^{-1}y^a x y^b x^{-1}y^c\bigr).
\end{equation}
For $n>0$ this is a Miller--Schupp presentation with
$w=y^a x y^b x^{-1}y^c$. The following dictionary records two
quotient-two specializations. A solution of the square pair in the right
column gives a solution of the original pair in the left column:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{Original tuple} & \text{Coordinate at }c=0 & \text{Square pair}\\ \hline
P(2s,s,p,c) & u=xy^{-p} & G(p,s)\\
P(2s+1,p,s+1,c) & u=xy^{-p} & G(p,s).
\end{array}
\]
Here $s,p$ are natural and $c$ is integral. The transports, including the
changes of basis, are witnessed by ordinary moves; their derivation is in
Section~\ref{sec:coordinates}. The first row at $s=0$ is still a well-defined
tuple even though the usual Miller--Schupp notation assumes $n>0$.

\subsection*{Relation to earlier constructions}
The relevant Miller--Schupp family is
\[
\operatorname{MS}(n,w)=\langle x,y\mid x^{-1}y^nx=y^{n+1},\ x=w\rangle,
\]
where $n>0$ and $w$ has exponent sum zero on $x$. Shehper et al.\ prove
AC-triviality for all $\operatorname{MS}(1,w)$, for all
$\operatorname{MS}(n,y^{-1}xyx^{-1})$, and for
$\operatorname{MS}(2,y^{-k}x^{-1}yxy)$ for every integer $k$
\cite[Theorem B; Theorems 2, 3 and Corollary 9]{shehper}.
Their substitution operation precedes the witness operations used here.
The $p=1$ slice enters their $\operatorname{MS}(1,w)$ class. Below we give
the root-family witnesses directly.

Miasnikov--Myasnikov prove AC-triviality for balanced trivial-group
presentations on two generators of total relator length at most twelve
\cite{mm}. Lisitsa uses automated theorem proving to obtain simplifications
of particular Miller--Schupp presentations and formulate parametric
conjectures \cite{lisitsa}. These provide finite classification and
computer-assisted construction precedents for the present uniform argument.
Bridson establishes lower bounds on AC complexity starting in rank four
\cite{bridson}. Our factor count measures one chosen rank-two construction;
it does not bound the length of a shortest trivialization from below.

Substitution by conjugate products and transport under automorphisms are
established techniques; see Panteleev--Ushakov \cite{pu}. Lackenby's ordinary
AC theorem for thickenable balanced presentations gives a different sufficient
condition \cite[Theorem 1.3]{lackenby}. We have not determined how the entire
family here intersects that geometric class. Lean formalization of AC and
automated certificate checking also have prior implementations
\cite{zhang,sair}. Our contribution is the arithmetic construction below
and its formal proof. Priority for the underlying triviality classes has
not been established by the comparison in this note.

\section{Witnesses that compile to ordinary moves}\label{sec:witness}
\begin{definition}
A witness relative to a relator $r$, from $a$ to $b$, is a finite ordered list
$W=((g_1,\epsilon_1),\ldots,(g_t,\epsilon_t))$, with $\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}$,
together with the identity in the ambient free group
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witness}
a\prod_{i=1}^t g_i r^{\epsilon_i}g_i^{-1}=b.
\end{equation}
We write $a\xRightarrow[r]{W}b$.
\end{definition}
This contains more data than an equality in the presented quotient.

\begin{lemma}[Witness compilation]\label{lem:compile}
An identity \eqref{eq:witness} gives $(a,r)\AC(b,r)$, with the second
relator restored after each factor. A positive factor costs three mathematical
primitives and a negative factor costs five. The analogous assertion holds
with the two slots exchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a positive factor, conjugate the donor $r$ by $g_i$, right multiply the
recipient, and conjugate the donor back by $g_i^{-1}$. For a negative factor,
invert the donor before the multiplication and invert it again afterward.
Repeat in the listed order.\end{proof}

The witness operations have explicit factor lists. Composition
concatenates factor lists. Conjugation by $h$ replaces every $g_i$ by $hg_i$.
Given $a\Rightarrow b$ and $c\Rightarrow d$ relative to the same donor,
a witness $ac\Rightarrow bd$ is obtained by replacing each conjugator in
the first list by $c^{-1}g_i$, then appending the second list. To obtain
$a^{-1}\Rightarrow b^{-1}$, reverse the list, negate all signs, and replace
each conjugator by $ag_i$. Iterating the product operation constructs
positive powers; inverse words give negative powers. These rules follow
directly by multiplying \eqref{eq:witness}.

\paragraph{Three counts, and official work.}
For a witness with $t_+$ positive factors and $t_-$ negative factors, the
compiler used here has the following costs. In the last row, $|g_i|$ is
the reduced letter length of the conjugator.
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Witness factors} & t=t_++t_-,\\[2pt]
\text{Mathematical primitives} & 3t_++5t_-,\\[2pt]
\text{Official numbered moves} &
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^t(2|g_i|+1)+2t_-.
\end{array}
\]
These are costs of compiling the factors, before any entry or finishing
moves. A numbered conjugation uses one generator letter; our negative-factor
compiler retains the two donor inversions. Other official paths can use
the numbered multiplication-by-inverse move directly. Identity conjugations
still count as mathematical primitives but emit no numbered moves.
For an exported path $R^{(0)},\ldots,R^{(L)}$, official work is
$\sum_{j=0}^{L} (|R^{(j)}_0|+|R^{(j)}_1|)$, with each word freely reduced.
The initial pair is included in this sum.
Thus 61 entry primitives, 81 primitives for a residual seed, and 183 or
21,819 numbered moves for complete examples measure different paths and
different operations. None is a shortestness assertion.

\section{Entry into a root-conjugacy pair}\label{sec:entry}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root}
B_p=y^{-p}u^2y^pu^{-1},\qquad
A_s=y^{-(s+1)}u^{-1}y^su^{-1}.
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}[Uniform entry]\label{lem:entry}
For all integers $p,s$, a route of 61 mathematical primitives takes $G(p,s)$
to $(f(B_p),f(A_s))$, where
\[
f(u)=y^{s+1-p}u^{-1}y^p,\qquad f(y)=y^{-1}.
\]
We have $\sol(G(p,s))$ if and only if $\sol((B_p,A_s))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The complete route is recorded in the artifact described in
Appendix~\ref{app:route}. After its first twelve
macros, which cost eighteen primitives, the pair is
\[
\bigl(y^{-p}u^{-1}y^pu^{-1}y^su,\quad
u y^{-p}u^{-2}y^pu y^{-1}\bigr).
\]
The remaining macros cost 43 primitives and end at the displayed image pair.
Each step is a free-group identity, checked in the accompanying
Lean files \texttt{ACDiagonalDescent} and \texttt{ACSquareP2Entry}.

The following identities explain the two donor substitutions in macros
13--51, in temporary bases related by the shears named below. They do not
by themselves specify that route: the full schedule, including the
normalizations and carried-back conjugators, is proved in
\texttt{ACSquareP2Entry}. Temporary changes of basis and cyclic
normalization put the first substitution
in the following form:
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_0&=u^{-2}y^{-p}u y^{-1}u y^p,&
T_0&=u^{-1}y^{-p}u y^s u^{-1}y^p,\\
R_1&=u^{-1}y^{-p}u y^{-(s+1)}u y^p.
\end{aligned}
\]
The relation $T_0$ replaces the second $u^{-1}$ in $R_0$ by
$y^{-p}u y^{-s}u^{-1}y^p$. The adjacent $u^{-1}u$ then cancels, leaving
$R_1$. More precisely,
\[
R_0 g_0 T_0^{-1}g_0^{-1}=R_1,
\qquad g_0=y^{-p}u^{-1}y u^{-1}y^p u.
\]
A shear $u\mapsto uy^p$ and normalization arrange the second substitution:
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_2&=u^{-2}y^{-p}u y^s,&
T_2&=u^{-1}y^{-p}u y^{p-s-1}u y^p,\\
R_3&=u^{-1}y^{-p}u^{-1}y^{-p+2s+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Now replace the second $u^{-1}$ in $R_2$ by
$y^{-p}u^{-1}y^{-p+s+1}u^{-1}y^p$. The exact factor is
\[
R_2 g_2 T_2^{-1}g_2^{-1}=R_3,
\qquad g_2=y^{-s}u^{-1}y^p u.
\]
After inverting the temporary generators, the shear
$u\mapsto uy^{s+1-p}$ exposes the root pair, up to cyclic normalization.
These changing bases explain the cancellations; they are not extra moves
in the claimed route. The frozen schedule carries all conjugators back
through the temporary bases and reaches the exact pair $(f(B_p),f(A_s))$.

To justify the solvability equivalence, the map $f$ is an automorphism:
its inverse sends
$u$ to $y^{-p}u^{-1}y^{p-s-1}$ and $y$ to $y^{-1}$.
A homomorphism sends every AC step to an AC step, with conjugators mapped
by that homomorphism. If $\gamma$ solves the root pair, the forward solution is
\[
G(p,s)\ \xrightarrow{\mathrm{entry}}\ f(B_p,A_s)
\ \xrightarrow{f(\gamma)}\ (f(u),f(y))
\ \xrightarrow{\beta_f}\ (u,y).
\]
Here $\beta_f$ is an ordinary basis correction:
conjugate its first relator by $y^{p-s-1}$, obtaining $u^{-1}y^{s+1}$;
right multiply it by the second relator to power $s+1$, then invert both
relators. For the converse, concatenate the reversed entry with a solution
of $G(p,s)$, apply $f^{-1}$ to that whole path, and correct its endpoint
$(f^{-1}(u),f^{-1}(y))$. Conjugation of the first relator by $y^p$ gives
$u^{-1}y^{-s-1}$; right multiplication by the second relator to power
$-s-1$, then two inversions, finishes. Each direction therefore transports
a complete path and supplies its own endpoint correction.
\end{proof}

\section{The two arithmetic finishes}\label{sec:finish}
\begin{lemma}[Iterated doubling]\label{lem:double}
For $p\in\Z$ and $m\in\N$, there is a witness relative to $B_p$
\[
y^{pm}u y^{-pm}\ \Longrightarrow\ u^{2^m}.
\]
The recursive list described here has $2^m-1$ factors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The basic witness from $y^puy^{-p}$ to $u^2$ has the single positive factor
with conjugator $y^pu^{-1}$; explicitly,
\[
(y^puy^{-p})(y^pu^{-1})B_p(y^pu^{-1})^{-1}=u^2.
\]
Start at $m=0$ with the empty witness. Write $W_m$ for the stage-$m$
list and put $d=2^m$. The induction has two stages, both relative to $B_p$:
\[
\begin{array}{c@{\qquad}l}
y^{p(m+1)}u y^{-p(m+1)} &\\[2pt]
\Big\Downarrow & \text{$W_m$ conjugated by $y^p$}\\[2pt]
y^p u^d y^{-p}=(y^puy^{-p})^d &\\[2pt]
\Big\Downarrow & \text{$d$ copies of the basic witness}\\[2pt]
(u^2)^d=u^{2d}. &
\end{array}
\]
In the second stage the product rule positions one copy at each of the $d$
factors; the donor is restored after every copy. This is the source of the
doubling growth. Conjugating a witness changes its conjugators but not its
number of factors, so $L_0=0$ and $L_{m+1}=L_m+2^m$. Summing this recurrence
gives $L_m=2^m-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Power finish]\label{lem:power}
For any integers $k,e$, $\sol((B_k,y^{-1}u^e))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With donor $q=y^{-1}u^e$, the identity $yq=u^e$ supplies a one-factor witness
replacing $y$ by $u^e$. Apply its power and product witnesses to
$B_k=y^{-k}u^2y^ku^{-1}$ to obtain $u$. The donor is restored. Right multiply
the second relator by the first to power $-e$ to obtain $y^{-1}$, then invert.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Arithmetic root families]\label{thm:root}
For all $p\in\Z$ and $m\in\N$, both
\[
\sol((B_p,A_{pm}))\quad\text{and}\quad
\sol((B_p,A_{pm+p-1}))
\]
hold by finite witness constructions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $d=2^m$. The purpose in each case is to produce a companion
$y^{-1}u^e$, while keeping a root relator available for the power finish.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Case 1: $s=pm$.}
Conjugate the companion by $y^{pm}$:
\[
y^{pm}A_{pm}y^{-pm}
=y^{-1}u^{-1}(y^{pm}u^{-1}y^{-pm}).
\]
Use the inverse-word witness of Lemma~\ref{lem:double} in the parenthesized
factor. This leaves $y^{-1}u^{-(d+1)}$, to which Lemma~\ref{lem:power} applies.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Case 2: $s=pm+p-1$.}
The same conjugation first gives
\[
y^{pm}A_{pm+p-1}y^{-pm}
=y^{-p}u^{-1}y^{p-1}(y^{pm}u^{-1}y^{-pm}).
\]
Applying the inverse doubling witness leaves the companion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
q=y^{-p}u^{-1}y^{p-1}u^{-d}.
\end{equation}
Set $v=u^{-1}y^{p-1}u^{-d}$.
The exact identity $y^pq=v$ is a one-factor witness relative to $q$.
Its inverse-word and product witnesses replace $y^p$ and $y^{-p}$ in
$B_p=(y^p)^{-1}u^2y^pu^{-1}$, while retaining $q$. The result is
\[
\begin{aligned}
v^{-1}u^2v u^{-1}
&=u^d y^{-(p-1)}u\,u^2u^{-1}y^{p-1}u^{-d}u^{-1}\\
&=u^d y^{-(p-1)}u^2y^{p-1}u^{-1}u^{-d}\\
&=u^d B_{p-1}u^{-d}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Only cancellation and commutation of powers of $u$ were used in the middle
line. Conjugate the first relator by $u^{-d}$ to obtain $(B_{p-1},q)$.
Thus the old companion becomes the donor for changing the first relator;
the resulting first relator becomes the donor for finishing the companion.
Conjugate $q$ by $y^{p-1}$ and rewrite it as
\[
y^{p-1}q y^{-(p-1)}
=y^{-1}u^{-1}(y^{p-1}u^{-d}y^{-(p-1)}).
\]
Since $(y^{p-1}uy^{-(p-1)})^{-d}
=y^{p-1}u^{-d}y^{-(p-1)}$, the $(-d)$-th power of the basic
degree-$(p-1)$ doubling witness changes the parenthesized block to $u^{-2d}$.
The companion is therefore $y^{-1}u^{-(2d+1)}$, as required for
Lemma~\ref{lem:power}. At each substitution,
Lemma~\ref{lem:compile} restores the donor, so the concatenation is a path
of ordinary rank-two moves.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
If $s=pm$ with $m\in\N$, use the first case of Theorem~\ref{thm:root} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:entry}. If $s+1=pk$ with $p,s,k\in\N$, then $k\geq1$;
set $m=k-1$, so $s=pm+p-1$, and use the second case. This includes the
degenerate $p=s=0$ through the first case.
\end{proof}

The same argument also proves $\sol(G(p,pm))$ and $\sol(G(p,pm+p-1))$
for arbitrary integer $p$ and natural $m$. Natural divisibility in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a convenient formulation with a nonnegative quotient.
The doubling construction can produce long certificates; no short-path or
polynomial-complexity claim follows from the theorem.

\section{Parameter consequences and the remaining induction}\label{sec:open}
We record the witnesses behind the parameter transports. To allow the two
complementary heights to vary separately, put
\[
S_N=u^2y^{-N},\qquad T_{p,s}=uy^pu y^{-s}u^{-1}y^{-p},\qquad
M_{p,s}=uy^pu y^{-p}u^{-1}y^{-s}.
\]
Thus $G(p,s)=(S_{2s+1},T_{p,s})$. Write
$\mathcal C_R(g)=gRg^{-1}$ for a conjugate of the donor $R$.
In the original coordinates $x,y$, the standard pair in the notation
$\sol$ is $(x,y)$.

\subsection{The original-coordinate transfers}\label{sec:coordinates}
\begin{lemma}[Quotient-two pinches]\label{lem:pinches}
Let $n\in\N$, $a,b\in\Z$, and $q=x^{-1}y^a x y^b x^{-1}$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
n=2a&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P(n,a,b,0)\AC\bigl((xy^{-b})^2y^{-(n+1)},q\bigr),\\
n+1=2b&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P(n,a,b,0)\AC\bigl((xy^{-a})^2y^{-n},q\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Each pinch uses two negative donor factors; the second also uses inversion
and conjugations of the first relator.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $a_0=x^{-1}y^a x$ and $z=xy^{-b}$. Since $q=a_0z^{-1}$,
\[
a_0\mathcal C_q(z^{-1})^{-1}=z.
\]
When $n=2a$, apply the square of this witness to
$a_0^2y^{-(n+1)}$. For the other pinch, use the one-factor identity
\[
(xy^b x^{-1})q^{-1}=y^{-a}x.
\]
If $n+1=2b$, invert the first relator and conjugate it by $x$, exposing
$(xy^b x^{-1})^2y^{-n}$. The squared witness gives
$(y^{-a}x)^2y^{-n}$, and conjugation by $y^a$ gives the stated endpoint.
Lemma~\ref{lem:compile} restores $q$ after each factor. These are the
\texttt{left\_pinch} and \texttt{right\_pinch} paths in
\texttt{ACSquareFamily}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Mirror and sign transport]\label{lem:mirror}
For all integers $N,p,s$,
\[
\sol((S_N,T_{p,s}))\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sol((S_N,M_{p,s}))\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sol((S_N,T_{-p,s})).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\iota(u)=u^{-1}$ and $\iota(y)=y^{-1}$, and put
$g=(uy^pu)^{-1}$. The free-group identities
\[
y^{-N}S_N^{-1}y^N=\iota(S_N),\qquad
gM_{p,s}^{-1}g^{-1}=\iota(T_{p,s})
\]
give a four-primitive path from $(S_N,M_{p,s})$ to
$\iota(S_N,T_{p,s})$. Transport a complete solution by $\iota$ and
invert both endpoint relators to correct $(u^{-1},y^{-1})$. Since
$\iota^2$ is the identity, this proves both directions of the first
equivalence.

For the second equivalence, put
\[
e=(uy^p)^{-1}M_{p,s}(uy^p)
  =uy^{-p}u^{-1}y^{-s}uy^p.
\]
The two-factor identity
\[
e\,\mathcal C_{S_N}(y^{-p}u^{-1}y^s)
  \mathcal C_{S_N}(y^{-p}u^{-1})^{-1}=T_{-p,s}
\]
gives an ordinary path of nine primitives from the mirror pair to the
negative-degree pair. Reversing this path completes the equivalence.
The corresponding formal paths are \texttt{mirror\_solvable} and
\texttt{mirror\_negative\_steps}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Complementary heights]\label{lem:complement}
For integers $p,s,k$, put $N=s+k$. There is a thirteen-primitive path
\[
(S_N,T_{p,s})\AC(S_N,T_{p,k}).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Invert the second relator and conjugate it by $u^{-1}$, obtaining
\[
e=u^{-1}T_{p,s}^{-1}u
 =u^{-1}y^pu y^su^{-1}y^{-p}.
\]
For $a=y^pu y^{-s}$, the required identity is
\[
e\,\mathcal C_{S_N}(au^{-1}y^{-p})
  \mathcal C_{S_N}(au^{-1})^{-1}
  \mathcal C_{S_N}(a)=T_{p,k}.
\]
The three factors cost eleven primitives; the initial inversion and
conjugation cost two. This is \texttt{complement\_steps}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Final-parameter shear]\label{lem:c-shear}
For $n\in\N$ and integers $a,b,c,t$,
\[
\sol(P(n,a,b,c))\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sol(P(n,a,b,c-t)).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\phi_t(x)=xy^t$ and $\phi_t(y)=y$. Conjugating both relators gives
\[
y^t\phi_t(P(n,a,b,c))y^{-t}=P(n,a,b,c-t),
\]
where conjugation of a tuple acts on each component. Starting at the
right-hand tuple, reverse those two conjugations, transport a solution of
$P(n,a,b,c)$ by $\phi_t$, and correct $(xy^t,y)$ by right multiplying
the first relator by the second to power $-t$. Reversing the shear gives
the converse. Equivalently, the left shear $x\mapsto y^tx$ sends the
original tuple directly to $P(n,a,b,c-t)$; the formal theorem
\texttt{ACMillerSchupp.shear\_family} uses this convention.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Deriving the dictionary.}
In the even row of Section~1, set $n=2s,a=s,b=p$ in
Lemma~\ref{lem:pinches}; in the odd row, set $n=2s+1,a=p,b=s+1$.
Both square relators become $S_{2s+1}$ in the coordinate $u=xy^{-p}$.
Inverting the donor $q$ and conjugating it by $x$ gives respectively
$M_{p,s}$ and $T_{p,s+1}$ in that coordinate. Lemma~\ref{lem:mirror}
handles the mirror; Lemma~\ref{lem:complement} exchanges $s+1$ and $s$.
The coordinate map sends $(u,y)$ to $(xy^{-p},y)$, whose first relator
is corrected by right multiplication by $y^p$. Finally,
Lemma~\ref{lem:c-shear} supplies every integral $c$. Consequently the
divisibility theorem applies to $P(2s,s,p,c)$ and $P(2s+1,p,s+1,c)$
whenever $p,s\in\N$ and $p\mid s$ or $p\mid(s+1)$.

\paragraph{An independent group calculation.}
Every $G(p,s)$, for integral $p,s$, presents the trivial group. Indeed,
in the presented group the second relation gives
$y^{-p}uy^p=uy^su^{-1}$. Squaring and using the first relation gives
\[
u^2=y^{-p}u^2y^p=(uy^su^{-1})^2=uy^{2s}u^{-1},
\qquad y^{2s}=u^2=y^{2s+1}.
\]
Thus $y=1$, and the second relation then gives $u^2=u$, so $u=1$.
This quotient-group calculation checks the defining relations; it supplies
no additional ordinary AC path.

\subsection{The period identity and covered residues}\label{sec:period}
\begin{lemma}[Four-factor period]\label{lem:period}
For integers $N,p,s$, the pair $(S_N,T_{p+N,s})$ reaches
$(S_N,T_{p,s})$ in sixteen mathematical primitives, with the square fixed.
In particular, for $s\in\Z$, solvability of $G(p,s)$ is periodic in $p$
with period $2s+1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_1&=y^{N+p}uy^su^{-1}y^{-N}, & g_2&=y^{N+p}uy^{s-N},\\
g_3&=y^{N+p}uy^{-N}, & g_4&=y^p.
\end{aligned}
\]
The explicit identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:period}
T_{p+N,s}\,
\mathcal C_{S_N}(g_1)\mathcal C_{S_N}(g_2)^{-1}
\mathcal C_{S_N}(g_3)\mathcal C_{S_N}(g_4)^{-1}=T_{p,s}.
\end{equation}
To derive it, first note the two-factor commutation witness, for any integer $t$:
\[
(y^Nu^t)\,\mathcal C_{S_N}(u^{-t}y^{-N})
\mathcal C_{S_N}(y^{-N})^{-1}=u^ty^N.
\]
The first factor replaces $y^N$ by $u^2$; the second replaces that square
back by $y^N$ after commuting it past $u^t$. Apply this with $t=1$ and
$t=-1$ to the middle word
\[
y^Nu y^{-s}u^{-1}\ \Longrightarrow\
uy^{-s}y^Nu^{-1}\ \Longrightarrow\ uy^{-s}u^{-1}y^N.
\]
Attach the prefix $uy^p$ and suffix $y^{-p-N}$. The product rule in
Section~\ref{sec:witness} gives exactly $g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4$ in that order.
Two positive and two negative factors cost $3+5+3+5=16$ primitives.
These are the factors of \texttt{squarePeriodWitness} in
\texttt{ACSquareDiagonalFamilies}, with $k=N-s$. Iterating the path or
its reverse proves the periodicity assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Covered residues]\label{cor:residues}
For $s\in\N$, $G(p,s)$ is AC-trivial whenever
\[
p\equiv0,\ \pm1,\ \pm2,\ \pm s\pmod{2s+1}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The cases $p=1,2,s$ follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; signs and periods
follow from Lemmas~\ref{lem:mirror} and~\ref{lem:period}.
For $p=0$, conjugate the second relator by $u^{-1}$ to obtain
$q=uy^{-s}$. The witness $u\,\mathcal C_q(u^{-1})^{-1}=y^s$
changes the first relator to $y^{-1}$. Invert the first relator, right
multiply the second by the first to power $s$, and exchange the two slots
to obtain $(u,y)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Heights three and four]\label{cor:heights}
Both $G(p,3)$ and $G(p,4)$ are AC-trivial for every integer $p$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The residues $0,\pm1,\pm2,\pm3$ exhaust the classes modulo seven.
At height four, Corollary~\ref{cor:residues} covers all classes modulo nine
except $\pm3$. The proved seed $G(3,4)$, its sign transport and its
periods cover those two classes.
\end{proof}
The seed is a finite kernel-checked certificate in \texttt{ACSquareP3Seed};
its 65 numbered moves start at the residual root pair associated with
$G(3,4)$, not at the square presentation itself. Its mathematical primitive
cost is 81. Thus the height-four result retains a finite seed as well as
the displayed uniform transports. By the dictionary, these two heights also
give $P(6,3,b,c)$, $P(7,a,4,c)$, $P(8,4,b,c)$ and $P(9,a,5,c)$ for all
remaining integral parameters. These are particular slices, not the full
three-parameter families at those exponents.

\subsection{What remains of exponent halving}
Here is the precise limitation of the proposed extension. Put
\[
Q_{r,d}=y^{-(r+1)}u^{-1}y^r u^{-d}.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Residual equivalence]\label{prop:residual}
For $p,r\in\Z$ and $m\in\N$,
\[
\sol(G(p,pm+r))\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sol((B_p,Q_{r,2^m})).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use Lemma~\ref{lem:entry}. Conjugating $A_{pm+r}$ by $y^{pm}$ gives
$y^{-(r+1)}u^{-1}y^r(y^{pm}u^{-1}y^{-pm})$.
The inverse-word doubling witness gives $Q_{r,2^m}$ while keeping $B_p$.
All these ordinary paths can be reversed.
\end{proof}

For the next residue $p=3,r=1$, a two-factor substitution
and invertible coordinate transport give the equivalent pair $(B_2,C_{2^m})$,
where
\[
C_d=y^{-1}u^{-1}y^{-1}u^{-1}y u^{-d},\qquad
D_d=y^{-3}u^{-1}y^{-1}u^{-1}y^3u^{-d}.
\]
Witnessed halving yields $(B_2,C_{2d})\AC(B_2,D_d)$, but the outer
$y$-exponents have changed. Closing the induction requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restoration}
(B_2,D_d)\AC(B_2,C_d),\qquad d=2,4,8,\ldots.
\end{equation}
The formalization proves that \eqref{eq:restoration} for all these $d$ is
equivalent to $\sol(G(3,3m+1))$ for every $m\in\N$, using the known
$m=0,1$ seeds. Neither side of this equivalence is proved here. In particular,
the first missing restoration is $d=2$; halving alone does not establish it.
This is a target for further work, and even its resolution would still leave
other square-family parameters to treat.

\section{Formal proof and executable evidence}\label{sec:repro}
The formalization uses Lean 4.29.1 and the pinned Mathlib revision in its Lake
manifest. Its copy of the official \texttt{AC.lean} is byte-identical to
the SAIR source \cite{sair} at commit
\begin{center}\small
\texttt{99a65377c5c4f412cd9af7b8d31c41464a855736}.
\end{center}
The main statement is
\texttt{AC.MillerSchupp.SquareFamily.P2.}\allowbreak
\texttt{divisibility\_square\_solvable}.
The root witness identity and both arithmetic finishes are quantified Lean
declarations.
Logical-dependency reports admit only the usual Lean axioms
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
The conjectures are definitions of propositions and are never assumed.

\paragraph{What is proved, and what is replayed.}
The Lean development proves the quantified AC constructions in this
paper. Its witness identities, entry path, arithmetic finishes and basis
corrections together establish the main declaration above in the official
definition of ordinary AC reachability. The theorem does not depend on a
Python execution or on the finite replay matrix.

The Python exporter is a separate implementation of the construction.
It specializes the fixed affine route, constructs the finite
witnesses, and expands them into the official ordinary rank-two move table.
An independent word interpreter maintains intermediate states, and the
unchanged SAIR verifier checks the final numbered path. The exporter also
handles the admissible zero case explicitly. The finite CLI has resource
bounds; those are not restrictions on the quantified Lean theorem.
The exporter is not extracted from Lean, and no theorem identifies its
complete factor lists with the Lean lists for every parameter. Agreement on
finitely many parameter values would not prove that identification.
An exported path is instead certified on its own by exact replay: each
numbered step is applied to the input pair and the standard endpoint is
checked. This gives reproducible finite certificates alongside the quantified
formal proof, with distinct verification responsibilities.

The experiment uses 50 distinct pairs: the 44 distinct pairs
$(p,pm)$ and $(p,pm+p-1)$ for $1\leq p\leq6$, $0\leq m\leq3$; all
$(2,s)$ for $0\leq s\leq12$; and $(0,0)$. All 50 pass unlimited official
replay. Of these, 48 satisfy the golden budgets of 100,000 numbered moves,
10,000 peak total relator length, and 5,000,000 work units. The exceptions
are $(2,11)$ and $(2,12)$, which exceed the work budget.
The recorded construction time was 2.980 seconds and replay time 0.567
seconds on the machine identified in \texttt{receipts/replay.json}; these
are one-run measurements, not portable performance guarantees.

\section{The affine schedule as a reproducibility artifact}\label{app:route}
The complete schedule is distributed as \texttt{entry\_route.json};
\texttt{paper/entry-table.tex} is its generated typeset listing. In that
listing, slots are indexed by $0,1$.
$I_i$ inverts relator $i$, $M_i^\epsilon$ replaces it by
$R_iR_{1-i}^\epsilon$, and $C_i(g)$ conjugates it by $g$.
An $M_i^{-1}$ entry expands into inversion of the donor, multiplication,
and inversion of the donor again. Apply the rows in order to $G(p,s)$.
Rows 1--12 reach the normal form in Lemma~\ref{lem:entry}; rows 13--51
reach $f(B_p,A_s)$. Thus the 51 macros comprise 61 primitives.
Section~\ref{sec:entry} explains the two donor substitutions and the
automorphism transport; the schedule fixes every intervening normalization.
\texttt{python3 paper/generate\_table.py} regenerates the listing.
The corresponding affine identities are proved for all integer $p,s$ in
the pinned Lean source. The package manifest records hashes for the JSON,
generated listing and formal source.
